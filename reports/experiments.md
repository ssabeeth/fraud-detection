# Experiments: what the data patterns suggested, and what held up

Generated by `fraud experiments`. Pre-registered in DECISIONS.md before the run, with the adoption rule. The same three folds as the model comparison score Feb, Mar, Apr once each; early stopping and calibration use the last 14 days of each training window; **May is not read**. Every experiment uses LightGBM with the same setting (num_leaves=63, min_child_samples=200, learning rate 0.1), so only the thing under test changes. Each experiment's pattern is the section of [data_patterns.md](data_patterns.md) that raised it.

Money is the cost of the untuned expected-loss policy (review whenever the expected saving is positive, within 50 a day). The interval for the saving resamples whole days within each month (1,000 draws). **Rule:** adopt only if cheaper in every month and the 95% interval of the pooled saving excludes zero.

## Findings

- **Without the 17 point-in-time aggregates**: saves $1,632 a month on average (95% interval -$4,925 to +$7,850 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by +0.001. Not adopted: cheaper in every month, but the interval includes zero.
- **Without the 339 V columns**: saves $3,509 a month on average (95% interval -$5,008 to +$12,418 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by -0.009. Not adopted: cheaper in every month, but the interval includes zero.
- **D columns normalised to dates (day − D)**: saves $6,024 a month on average (95% interval -$4,355 to +$15,713 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by +0.015. Not adopted: cheaper in 2 of 3 months.
- **Plus per-card means of earlier C and normalised D values**: saves $7,741 a month on average (95% interval -$617 to +$16,707 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by +0.003. Not adopted: cheaper in every month, but the interval includes zero.
- **Plus the card's chargebacks known after the 30-day delay**: saves $49,176 a month on average (95% interval +$40,023 to +$59,372 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by +0.095. **Adopted**: cheaper in all 3 months, interval above zero.
- **Normalised D, card means and label history together**: saves $44,476 a month on average (95% interval +$32,609 to +$55,644 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by +0.092. **Adopted**: cheaper in all 3 months, interval above zero.
- **Without the 10 most time-dependent features**: saves $701 a month on average (95% interval -$7,485 to +$9,377 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by +0.004. Not adopted: cheaper in 2 of 3 months.
- **Trained on the latest 60 days only**: costs $6,789 a month on average (95% interval -$14,910 to +$1,641 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by -0.009. Not adopted: cheaper in 1 of 3 months.
- **Fraud weighted five times**: costs $4,810 a month on average (95% interval -$15,863 to +$5,523 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by -0.011. Not adopted: cheaper in 1 of 3 months.
- **Current features plus a block list (decline cards with a known chargeback)**: saves $34,716 a month on average (95% interval +$27,682 to +$40,988 saved a month) and changes mean PR-AUC by -0.080. Post hoc, added after the results were seen, so not eligible for adoption.

## What changes

2 changes pass the rule, and they overlap. **Plus the card's chargebacks known after the 30-day delay** saves the most over the three months (+$147,529, against +$133,428 for "Normalised D, card means and label history together") with 455 features, so it alone is adopted. The rule did not say what to do when overlapping changes both pass; this tie-break (most money saved, then fewest features) was decided after the results were seen, and the other additions each fail the rule on their own.
It is then built in the Spark and stream feature code with point-in-time and parity tests, the model is retrained and tuned on April by the usual pipeline, the policy re-frozen, and May scored once more as a new, logged result.

## Is it more than a block list?

Vesta's labels mark transactions that follow a reported chargeback on the same card as fraud too, so a model that knows about earlier chargebacks is partly learning that labelling rule. In production the rule's equivalent is a block list. So the post-hoc question is whether the model does better than the current model with a block list that declines any card with a known chargeback.

The block list alone saves +$104,148 over the three months (+$30,631 / +$41,642 / +$31,875). The model with the label history saves a further +$43,380 on top of it (+$4,637 / +$10,749 / +$27,994; 95% interval +$20,408 to +$66,860), cheaper in all 3 months: it learns more from the history than the rule does.
