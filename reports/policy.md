## The cost model

From `configs/costs.yaml`; each value has its rationale there and in DECISIONS.md.

- **Approve**: a fraud costs the amount plus the chargeback fee.
- **Decline**: a legitimate customer costs the lost margin (a share of the amount) plus a fixed amount.
- **Review**: costs the analyst's time; the analyst stops a share of the fraud; a legitimate order is approved late at a small cost.
- **Capacity**: at most 50 reviews a day, allocated in arrival order: the queue cannot see the rest of the day, so a policy that wastes early slots on low-value cases has none left for large ones.

## Ranking for review: expected saving against probability (validation)

Both rankings get the same number of reviews: the top 50 of each day with hindsight over the day (so the comparison is about the ranking alone, not the queueing), everything else approved,
no declines. LightGBM's calibrated probabilities.

| Review the daily top by | Total cost | Fraud value caught | Reviews that were fraud |
|---|---|---|---|
| expected saving | $294,035 | 46.8% | 786 of 1,500 |
| probability | $347,190 | 33.3% | 1,160 of 1,500 |

The expected saving is P × (amount + fee) × catch rate − review cost − (1 − P) × delay cost: the brief's P(fraud) × amount − review cost, plus the fee, the analyst's misses and the delay. 
Ranking by it costs $53,155 less over the month than ranking by probability. It sends fewer actual frauds to review but larger ones: a 60% chance on a $2,000 order is worth reviewing before a 90% chance on a $20 one.
